Improving the Claims Experience :
Research backed UX

Minimising user pain points through streamlined, optimised flows while placing a strong emphasis on a mobile-first strategy to enhance task completion & user satisfaction.

Outcome

Through a UX audit and flow analysis, I identified excessive steps that could be simplified, combined, or systemised—leading to meaningful gains in user experience.
Usability testing confirmed faster task completion and revealed missing functionality, which once addressed, contributed to higher user satisfaction with online claims.
Post-release, claims-related call and chat volume dropped, and user retention increased.

Background

Asurion Home+ is a device protection and insurance service with a customer-facing marketing site and a portal app for managing devices and claims. I led design for Sprints 1–3, delivering core product flows under shifting requirements and tight timelines. In Sprint 4, I onboarded two additional designers.

The project was partially localized from a US-led, web-first design, requiring adaptation for regional needs while building a scalable experience.

Requirements

The online claims experience is one of four key flows in Asurion Home+. CX research and prior usability testing highlighted five areas for improvement.

  1. Enhanced Problem Entry UI

    Users must be able to select predefined device issue categories and optionally provide additional details via a free-text field.

  2. Refined Compensation Options

    Compensation choices shall display clear, contextual information to support informed decision-making.

  3. Step-By-Step Guidance

    The workflow must include a visible progress indicator and contextual prompts for each stage.

  4. Multiple Contact Channels

    The interface shall surface phone, chat, and email support methods for user convenience.

  5. Streamlined Interface

    Non-essential fields and steps shall be minimized or hidden to reduce cognitive load.

Process & Strategy

Discovery Phase

Careful analysis of the user flow led to key enhancements in how users navigated the claims process. Decision points were simplified by:

  • Automated Selections

    Convert manual selections into system-driven actions to reduce user effort and improve consistency.

  • Optimized Decision Flows

    Use a system matrix to determine the correct flow and break complex decisions into shorter, manageable steps.

  • Information Hierarchy

    Apply a clear hierarchy to information to prioritize relevant content and guide user understanding.

Prioritising a Mobile First Design Approach

There was some initial fatigue after the first release of the service and I was aware that design debt had crept into the product with some of that being a result of following a web first design approach. Using the period of rest I decided to lead my design improvements from a mobile first perspective which with some soft skill negotiating could allow design to lead product improvements from a UX standpoint.

Key Improvements

Repair as singular flow

Quick and direct flow for majority of users with additional scope for date, time entry

Improving Problem Identification

Increased selection options which allowed better system recognition and limited further questioning

Replacement with upgrade integration

Prioritised replacement and upgrade options with visual aids to engage the users decision process.

Solutions

Improving Problem Identification

Initial input relied on outdated desktop date selectors and free-text problem entry. Failure in system recognition triggered up to three follow-up screens, increasing user friction and cognitive load.

My solution was to collapse the multi-step process into a clean, mobile-optimized selection tree while retaining the existing text entry option. By improving input precision, the system could better recognize user issues with fewer prompts, making the flow faster and more intuitive.

I also proposed voice-to-text input to improve accessibility and ease of use, and modernized the date selector with quick options like “Today” and “Yesterday” for faster interaction.

Replacement with upgrade integration

With three compensation options—Repair, Replace, and Reimburse—and separate criteria influencing each, the experience risked overwhelming users and creating complexity as new requirements emerged.
Since repair was the most efficient and frequently used method, I proposed a system matrix based on device type and issue. This would enable the system to recommend the most suitable option while allowing repair and replacement to operate as independent flows.

Stakeholder alignment with Product Upgrade

Having previously partnered with senior management to develop upgrade feature concepts aimed at retention, I saw this task as an opportunity to quietly bring those ideas back into play.
Despite device imagery being out of scope, I felt it was key to making the experience more visually compelling and emotionally engaging—especially in encouraging users to upgrade aging devices. The UI was influenced by Amazon’s “Try Before You Buy,” tailored to our service model.

Usability Testing

Additional Usability Testing

After initial discovery and ideation, I aligned with both my design manager and the product manager to move forward with proposed UX improvements. The ideas were well received, and since the claims flow hadn’t been fully tested end-to-end, we included them in the next usability testing round. Previous sessions had focused on desktop UI. This time, I wanted to explore mobile preference, validate the new condensed problem entry, and assess overall task clarity across the redesigned flow.

Testing Criteria

  • Preference for mobile-first vs desktop interface
  • Effectiveness of condensed problem entry versus multi-step fallback
  • Clarity of compensation option selection and supporting information
  • User perception of upgrade integration within the replacement flow
  • Overall task load and confidence at flow completion

Insights & Next Steps

Usability testing strongly validated key improvements—particularly the mobile-first flow and condensed problem entry, which reduced friction and improved completion rates. Most participants found the updated experience easier to follow, with fewer points of confusion compared to the original design.

  1. In-Flow Guidance

    Medium-to-low literacy and senior users preferred stronger, step-by-step guidance with simplified language and clearer progress indicators.

  2. Alternative Contact Options

    Users requested easier access to phone or chat support during moments of confusion, particularly when encountering blocked or unclear steps.

  3. Confirmation & Confidence

    Clear confirmation at the end of the flow was essential. Users wanted visual reassurance that their claim had been submitted successfully.

  4. Access to Appointments

    Users expected an easy way to review or manage their upcoming repair appointments directly from the confirmation screen or dashboard.

With these insights in mind, I moved into refining the design work—focusing on clarity, accessibility, and confidence at each step of the flow. My goal was to ensure the claims experience not only performed well in ideal cases but also supported users who needed more guidance, reassurance, or direct support.

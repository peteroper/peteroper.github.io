Content Design :
Turning Proactive Iteration into Strategic Alignment

A post-release design initiative that began as self-driven improvement, and evolved into a key reference for a future project.

Seeing Potential Others Overlook

This experience reminded me that design is often a bridge between status quo and opportunity. Sometimes you see clear potential in a product, but the energy to improve it just isn’t there—yet. In those moments, it’s easy to move on. But I’ve learned that with the right mix of timing, persistence, and clear communication, ideas can find their moment.

Even when a solution isn’t implemented right away, putting in the work—framing the problem well, exploring real alternatives, and validating with users—can quietly shift the conversation. That’s exactly what happened here. What started as a missed opportunity turned into a chance to reframe the future of the product with qualitative data to support it.

The Challenge

A Shifting Scope and a Missed Opportunity

I initially led the design of a new content experience aimed at boosting engagement and encouraging daily interaction. The vision was exciting—introducing video and manga content, gamifying exploration, and grouping content into thematic collections to enhance discoverability and completion.

However, once the discovery phase concluded, the scope began to shift. Requirements changed midstream, which led to compromises in structure and missed opportunities to rethink how content *should* be presented. While the UI appeared fresh, it leaned too heavily on legacy templates and lacked the strategic evolution the experience deserved.

Reframing Post-Launch

Jakob’s Law and Building on Familiarity

After launch, I proactively revisited the experience—this time guided by the final, locked requirements. I looked to familiar UI patterns from leading apps like Netflix and top ed-tech platforms, analyzing how they structured content to encourage exploration and repeat use.

I used Jakob’s Law as a core tenet during the desktop research phase. The principle suggests that users prefer interfaces that mirror what they already know, as it reduces learning curves and cognitive effort.

Through competitive analysis, I identified patterns users already trust—such as like personalized content rows, smart grouping, and intuitive progress tracking—I had a foundation to make daily interaction effortless and rewarding.

Design Advocacy

Turning Research into Influence

I made presentation slides highlighting best practices in UI found across the different apps along with areas which might not translate well for our user base. I then took this inspiration and started to align with existing locked in requirements and built a refined UI with a strong focus on engagement, pick up daily content and discoverability.

Over the following weeks, I maintained regular dialogue with the main PM on the task and we reflected on missed opportunities. When the opportunity came, I did a simple walkthrough of both the presentation and revised design concepts—delivering them at a time when the team was open to what if type discussions.

While we couldn’t immediately move forward with the updated direction, the ideas resonated. I later learned that the concepts remained top-of-mind for the product manager.

Strategic Alignment

When Old Ideas Find New Life

Later in the project, a new content feature entered the strategy phase with limited time to explore fresh directions. The team revisited my earlier design work, and one of my concepts was picked for usability testing to help guide the approach.

We tested it alongside the original version, focusing on how users navigated, understood the structure, and whether they felt encouraged to come back daily. I created straightforward tasks and quick prototypes to keep things focused

Usability Testing

Putting Ideas to the Test

We tested both designs with users across three key focus areas:

  • Navigation effectiveness – How easily could users explore and move through grouped content?
  • Structural clarity and learning – Could users understand how the content was organized and what to do next?
  • Likelihood of daily engagement – Did the new layout and visual cues make users feel motivated to return?

The redesigned version outperformed the original across all criteria—especially in navigation and user confidence. Users reported a stronger sense of direction and intent, and more participants said they would explore the app daily thanks to the clearer structure and goal-based presentation.

Client Engagement

Turning Caution into Confidence

Our client, KDDI, had been a cautious partner throughout my time working on the app. With a long-standing legacy product, there was hesitancy around change—particularly visual or interaction shifts that could impact long-time users.

However, seeing a fully realized, modern UI paired with strong usability feedback shifted the conversation. The test results showed that not only could users navigate the new experience more confidently, but they also preferred it—and that daily usage might actually improve with the new model.

Armed with this validation, stakeholders at KDDI became more open to change. What began as skepticism turned into curiosity, then alignment. The design didn’t just resonate—it became a catalyst for broader conversations around modernizing the app's overall user experience.

This case study is a snapshot — I’ve kept it focused to balance work, life, and clarity.
